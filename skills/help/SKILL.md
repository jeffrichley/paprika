# What she can ask for

Answer in **plain sentences about jobs**, never a list of commands. She should
finish knowing what to say next, not what to type.

Say the handful that are actually useful to her, in her words:

> I can plan a week of dinners from your own recipes, put the shopping list in
> Paprika with what you've already got taken off, get recipes in from a photo or
> a link, find something you half-remember, and tidy up a library that's got
> messy. Just say what you want in your own words.

## Rules

- **Never list command names.** She does not type them and should not learn them.
- **Never enumerate all eleven jobs.** Four or five, chosen for her.
- **If she asked about one thing, answer that**, and stop. Do not tour.
- **If setup is not finished, say so once** and point at `/paprika:setup`.
- **If something is genuinely not possible, say so plainly** rather than
  offering the nearest thing as though it were what she asked for.

## What this cannot do

Worth saying when asked, because the boundary is deliberate: it does not help
while she is cooking, does not merge duplicate recipes for her, and does not
give micronutrients. Nutrition is four numbers, by the week, only when asked.
